# Report Issues

## Overview

**Help & Support** in your sidebar has three tabs: **Knowledge Base**, **Report an Issue**, and **My Issues**. Between them they cover reading up on a problem, telling your teacher about one, and finding out what happened next.

Before you report anything, check **Knowledge Base**. It has **How To Guides** and **Troubleshooting** articles, and a lot of "it's broken" turns out to be "I didn't know where the button was."

## Step-by-step instructions

### Which kind of report to file

There are two, and picking the right one matters more than anything else you write.

| Your problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| A specific transaction, payroll entry, or attendance record is wrong | Report it **from the record itself** |
| The app is broken — errors, blank pages, a button that does nothing | File a **General Issue** |

The page says it plainly: *Reports about transactions and attendance records can be made directly from where the log is located.*

### Reporting a specific record

Find the entry — in your transaction list, payroll history, or attendance log — and choose the **help** icon next to it.

This is worth doing properly rather than filing a general issue instead. The report arrives with the amount, account, description, and date already attached, and your teacher sees *You are creating support ticket for this transaction* with those details filled in. They do not have to go hunting for which charge you meant.

### Filing a general issue

1. Open the **Report an Issue** tab.
2. Choose **Report General Issue**. The card describes when to use it: *When the app is not working correctly such as giving error messages or opening a blank page.*
3. Pick an **Issue Type** from the dropdown. *Select the type of issue you're experiencing.*
4. Fill in **What happened?** — the placeholder asks you to *Describe what happened. Be specific!*
5. Optionally fill in **What did you expect to happen?**
6. If the app noticed something go wrong, you may see *We detected a recent error. Include it with this ticket.* Leave it ticked.
7. Choose **Submit Issue**.

The form gives you its own advice, which is good advice: be specific about what went wrong, include what you were trying to do, mention when it happened, and explain what you expected instead.

### Following your report

**My Issues** lists everything you have submitted, newest first, with the issue type, a status badge, the first part of what you wrote, and when you sent it. A **Related record** badge means it was filed against a specific transaction.

There are six statuses:

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Submitted** | Filed. Your teacher has not opened it yet. |
| **Teacher Review** | Your teacher is looking at it. |
| **Escalated to Developer** | Your teacher could not fix it and sent it on to the people who build the app. |
| **Developer Fix Applied - Teacher Review Required** | The app was fixed. Your teacher is checking the result. |
| **Teacher Final Review** | Nearly done — your teacher is closing it out. |
| **Closed** | Finished. |

Before you file anything the tab reads *You haven't submitted any issues yet.*

## Important notes

> [!WARNING]
> **You cannot edit or delete a report once it is sent.** The form warns you: *Once you submit this form, you will not be able to edit or delete this issue. Be sure to double-check the information you are providing is accurate before submitting your ticket.* Read yours back before choosing Submit.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **"It doesn't work" cannot be fixed.** Your teacher only sees what you typed. Compare *the store is broken* with *when I press Purchase on the Blue Pencil, the page goes blank and my balance does not change* — the second one can be acted on today.

> [!NOTE]
> **Escalation is not your teacher ignoring you.** **Escalated to Developer** means the problem was real and bigger than your classroom. If you have found a genuine bug, you can receive a reward for it.

> [!TIP]
> The counter under **What did you expect to happen?** counts up to 1000, but that field is actually limited to 500 characters — a longer answer is rejected when you submit. Keep it to a couple of sentences and you will not hit it.
